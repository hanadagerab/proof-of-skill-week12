## Scoring anchors
- Human harm: 1 cosmetic inconvenience; 3 meaningful wasted time or misleading description; 5 confidential disclosure, inaccurate consequential claim or exclusion.
- Threat to value: 1 local annoyance; 3 material friction; 5 defeats truthful self-representation or makes candidate worse off than existing tools.
- Evidence strength: 1 researcher impression; 3 repeated observable behavior; 5 convergent observed task failure plus participant evidence/verified artifact. Never invent quote/support.
- Frequency: 1 one occurrence; 2 two; 3 three; 4 four; 5 all five completed sessions. Record refusals explicitly.
- Feasibility: 1 unbounded/high privacy risk; 3 narrow measured fix; 5 simple reversible change with tests.

## Decision rule
Rank by human harm and threat first, then strength and frequency; feasibility is a tie-breaker and execution constraint, not a license to optimize the easiest issue. Preserve raw scores and dissent; never replace judgment with an opaque weighted score. Choose the **three most serious** observed problems. Select only the highest justified fix after review; if the competitor wins or risk remains unacceptable, do not code.
